Is the Orlando Informer Meetup Worth It for Families? Our Full Review, Plus What You Need to Know About Epic Universe

June 30, 2026  ·  10 min read  ·  By Jordan Roberson
← Back to Blog

We have now attended the Orlando Informer Meetup three times with our kids across Summer 2025, Winter 2025, and June 2026. Every single time, we walk away saying the same thing: this event delivers. Here is everything you need to know before you decide if it is right for your family.

What Is the Orlando Informer Meetup?

If you have never heard of it, the Orlando Informer Meetup is a privately ticketed event held at Universal Orlando. It is not a membership and it is not a fan convention. It is a two-night exclusive experience inside the actual Universal theme parks with lower crowds, unlimited food at participating restaurants, character meet and greets, and access to the rides without the lines you would normally deal with on a regular park day.

The event typically runs on a Friday and Saturday night each season and wraps up at 12:30 AM. Here is how the timeline actually works, because understanding this changes how you plan your night.

Check-in opens at 3 PM at Arrival Bytes. You pick up your wristband and credentials there, enjoy the entertainment and early refreshments, then head into the parks alongside regular day guests. During that first hour or so before park close, your wristband gets you access to designated free food spots inside the park while everything else is still running as normal. Think of it as a warm-up window. Then, at park close, the day guests leave and the exclusive event fully kicks in. That is when the unlimited food at all participating restaurants opens up, the crowds drop dramatically, and the parks feel like yours.

When you factor in the full window from 3 PM check-in to 12:30 AM, you are getting close to the same amount of park time as a standard day ticket. The parks typically stay open about ten hours on a regular day, so you are really only giving up about two hours on the early end, and you are gaining a night experience that most families never get to have. They run multiple weekends per season, which means the crowds are naturally split across dates. This is one of the smartest things about how the event is structured.

Arrival Bytes: How the Night Starts

One of the things that sets this event apart from just buying a park ticket is how it begins. Check-in happens at a dedicated area called Arrival Bytes, and it is genuinely a whole experience on its own. You pick up your credentials and event wristbands, but there is also live entertainment, refreshments, and meet and greet time with Churro, the Orlando Informer mascot.

Our kids absolutely love Churro. That big blue bird has become a highlight we look forward to every time. And meeting him here, at the start of the night, before you even step foot in the park, sets the tone for everything that follows. It feels less like buying a ticket and more like being welcomed into something exclusive.

Roberson Family Tip

Check-in officially opens at 3 PM. Do not sleep on this. The earlier you arrive, the more time you have before and during the event. We checked in a little later during the June 2026 event due to a rain delay and still had a great night, but getting there at or near 3 PM gives you the most runway. And before you go: read every email Orlando Informer sends you and keep the official Orlando Informer app handy during the event. Both are loaded with up-to-date info on food locations, character schedules, show times, and anything that shifts on the night.

The Food Value: This Is Where the Math Gets Real

The unlimited food is the single biggest financial argument for this event, and we want to walk through the math so it actually lands for you.

On a typical day at Universal, our crew budgets roughly $70 to $80 per meal at a sit-down restaurant. That is one meal. At the Orlando Informer Meetup, the food is unlimited across all participating restaurants throughout the night. You can eat at Burger Digs, Thunder Falls, Circus McGurkus, the Three Broomsticks, and more, and there is no cap on what you order. Each visit counts as its own experience.

At the June 2026 event, we hit Burger Digs first and tried the new Velociraptor tart dessert. We ordered multiples because we could and because it was that good. Then we made our way over to Thunder Falls and had what we will confidently say was some of the best grilled chicken and ribs we have ever eaten at a theme park. The food at Orlando Informer is prepared fresh for the event. It is the same park food you know, just made at its absolute best.

At the Summer 2025 event, we were at Circus McGurkus and the spread was incredible. Roast beef sliders, chicken and waffles, and a cream-filled dessert that we are still thinking about. We also kept the butterbeer flowing all night, which adds up fast at about $7 a cup on a normal day.

Add all of that up across a family, across multiple restaurant stops, and the math is not close. The food alone regularly justifies the ticket price for us before we even get on a single ride.

Pro Tip

The food menu changes with each season. Summer events lean into warm-weather comfort food and themed desserts. Winter events bring holiday-specific items, hot drinks, and festive treats like the Grinch cookies we absolutely demolished at the Winter 2025 event. Check the Orlando Informer event guide when you buy your tickets so you know what to prioritize.

The Rides: A Different Park Entirely

The best way to describe the ride experience at Orlando Informer is this: you get to do the things you normally skip. When you are at Universal on a regular day with kids, you are making constant trade-offs between wait times, the heat, and energy levels. Spider-Man might have a 45-minute wait. You skip it. Hagrid's might be an hour and a half. You debate it. You move on.

At this event, that calculus changes completely. Spider-Man was a walk-on. We went straight through the Express entrance and saw parts of the queue area we had never noticed before. That alone felt like a backstage tour. We have been coming to these parks for years and still discovered something new.

And then there is Hagrid's. As of July 1, 2026, Universal ended Express Pass access for Hagrid's Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure. That is the most popular ride at Islands of Adventure and now there is no way to skip the line on a regular day ticket. At the Orlando Informer Meetup, that does not apply. We made Hagrid's our first stop of the June 2026 night, and the posted wait was only 20 minutes all night long. We personally waited about 15 minutes. Our daughters Aria and Alexa rode it for the first time, and experiencing that together without battling a 90-minute line is something a standard park day just could not have given us.

Hagrid's Express Pass Alert

Starting July 1, 2026, Universal ended Express Pass access for Hagrid's Magical Creatures Motorbike Adventure. On a regular day that ride can sit at 60 to 90 minutes with no way to skip it. At the June 2026 Orlando Informer Meetup, it posted 20 minutes all night and we were on in about 15. Make it your first stop.

The key to maximizing your ride time at this event is flexibility. Do not get married to any one ride. If something is taking more than ten minutes, move on and come back. With two parks available and a 9-plus hour event window, you have more time than you think. The families who struggle are the ones who spend an hour waiting on one attraction and then feel behind all night. The families who thrive treat it more like a stroll than a sprint.

Universal also has the best child swap system of any theme park we have visited. Dedicated swap rooms, clear process, and they keep your group as together as possible throughout. If you have little ones who are not quite at height requirements yet, do not let that stop you from attending. The child swap setup makes it work for everyone.

Character Meet and Greets With Zero Line

At Orlando Informer, meeting a character is a walk-up. No line. You just go. On a regular park day, the ride lines for attractions in those same areas can run 30 to 45 minutes and that alone eats into the time you might have spent at a character meet and greet. Here, that trade-off does not exist. We have met Spider-Man at both the Summer 2025 and June 2026 events and both times it was immediate, with no crowd pressure and no rush. The kids got actual time with the character, not just a quick photo and move along.

The character lineup shifts with each season and event. During the Summer events you get the expected Universal characters. At the Winter 2025 Meetup, the Grinch had his own meet and greet, and the holiday version of Churro was out in Universal Studios. Those are experiences that, during the actual Universal Holiday Celebration, come with real crowds and long waits eating into your day. At Orlando Informer, you catch the same characters by walking straight up to them.

If character meet and greets are something your kids care about, this is the version of Universal where that actually happens in a way that feels real and memorable, not rushed and transactional.

Summer vs. Winter: Which Event Is Right for You?

We have done both seasons now and they offer genuinely different experiences. Here is how we break it down.

The Summer Meetup leans into the theme park experience at its purest. More ride-focused, summer-specific food menus, and great for families who want to maximize their park time with kids who are old enough to ride. The summer crowd management is excellent because the event is spread across multiple weekends.

The Winter Meetup adds a layer of magic on top of everything. Daigon Alley at Christmas is one of the most beautifully decorated spaces we have ever walked through at any theme park. The holiday shows have special overlays. The food leans festive. The characters are in holiday outfits. And doing all of this at Orlando Informer rather than during the actual Universal Holiday Celebration means you are experiencing it without the crowds, the premium ticket prices, and the exhausting lines that come with the official event.

Our take: if your kids are young and the magic of a lit-up theme park at Christmas is something they are ready to feel, do the Winter event. If you want the most family-friendly, kid-optimized, ride-heavy night possible, go Summer. If you can swing both, that is the real answer.

One Night vs. Two: You Need to Know This Before You Buy

We have done this event both ways, and we want to be real with you: one night is not enough to do everything.

The event gives you unlimited food and access to the rides, and the honest reality is that trying to prioritize both in a single night means you will feel like you are constantly choosing. Do we eat at one more restaurant or go do another ride? Do we wait for the character meet and greet or get back on Spider-Man? That tension never fully goes away in one night.

The two-night approach solves this. Use night one to focus on rides. Use night two to slow down, eat at every spot you want to hit, catch the shows, take in the atmosphere, and let the kids run the agenda. You stop rushing and start actually enjoying the event the way it is meant to be experienced.

At the June 2026 event we did one night and had a great time, but even then we said it out loud: two nights is the move. If budget allows, do it. The per-night rate drops when you buy a multi-night pass, so the math actually gets better when you add the second night.

We know that price tag is the first thing families see and it gives some people pause. But let us look at what you are actually comparing it to.

A standard one-day Universal park ticket with park-to-park access runs well over $150 per person before you touch a single piece of food. Add Express Pass and you are adding another $80 to $200 per person depending on the day. Then add food for your crew across the day, and you are well above what an Orlando Informer ticket costs, for a regular crowded park day with no exclusive access and no unlimited food.

The Orlando Informer ticket covers your park admission and unlimited food from 3 PM to 12:30 AM with low crowds and no extra fees at the gate. When you put it next to a real-cost day ticket, the value proposition is not even close.

Our verdict for out-of-town families visiting Universal: this is a no-brainer. If you are spending money on a park day anyway, there is almost no scenario where the Orlando Informer Meetup does not offer more value. For locals with annual passes, it is still worth it for the food alone, the exclusive access, and the experience of seeing the parks in a way that a regular visit just cannot match.

Buy Early and Save

Orlando Informer typically releases early bird pricing leading up to each event. Buying early can save you between $30 and $85 per ticket compared to the door price. Keep an eye on OrlandoInformer.com and their email list for discount announcements. Prices increase as the event fills up, so the longer you wait, the more you pay.

The Hotel Hack That Makes the Night Even Better

One of our favorite moves at the Orlando Informer Meetup is what we call the hotel drop. After a few hours in the parks with the younger kids, we put them in the stroller, walk over to our Universal resort hotel, drop them off with a trusted adult, and then head back in for the rest of the night.

At the June 2026 event we stayed at Endless Summer Dockside and the round trip from park to hotel and back took about 30 minutes. That is it. And what you get on the other side is two to three hours in the parks with just you and your partner or your older kids, moving freely, doing things that are harder to do with a stroller or a toddler in tow. We knocked out four rides and hit two more restaurants in that window.

At a previous event we used Cabana Bay for the same move. Same result. The proximity of the Universal resort hotels to the parks is a genuine advantage at this event, and if you are attending, we highly recommend staying on-site for exactly this reason.

Epic Universe Is Next and We Cannot Wait

Orlando Informer just announced the first ever Meetup at Epic Universe, and it is happening November 13, 2026. We cannot overstate how big of a deal this is.

Epic Universe is the most anticipated theme park addition in a generation. We have been there twice now and it is everything the hype promised. The idea of experiencing that park at an Orlando Informer event with low crowds, unlimited food across Epic Universe restaurants, and no line at Harry Potter and the Battle at the Ministry is genuinely exciting to think about.

Important: Epic Universe Access

Epic Universe is included on November 13 only, and you must purchase the two-night ticket package to access it. The two-night package (November 13 at Epic Universe + November 14 at Islands of Adventure and Universal Studios Florida) runs $450 plus taxes and fees. A single-night ticket for November 14 only covers the original two parks at $254. If Epic Universe is the reason you are going, you need the two-night package. Check OrlandoInformer.com for current availability and pricing as tickets do sell out.

We will be there. And we will have the full review ready for you when we get back. Stay tuned.

Family Pro Tips Before You Go

  • Download the Orlando Informer App before you arrive. The app is your personal road map for the entire event. Inside you will find an interactive park map, the full food directory broken down by restaurant, character meet and greet locations and times, show schedules, and real-time updates if anything shifts on the night. It tells you exactly where to go and when, so you are not wandering around trying to figure out where the Grinch cookies are or when Spider-Man is out. You can also text the Orlando Informer team directly through the app with any question and get a real answer fast. Do not show up without it.
  • You can text them questions. The Orlando Informer team is reachable by text during the event. If you cannot find something or want to know where to go next, just ask. That kind of access to real support during a theme park event is rare.
  • Be flexible with rides. If a ride or character line is taking more than ten minutes, move on and come back later. There is enough time in the event window to do the things you want, but only if you do not lose an hour waiting on one thing.
  • Prioritize Hagrid's first. Go straight there when the parks open. With Express Pass now gone for that attraction on regular days, the Orlando Informer event is one of the best ways to experience it without a painful wait.
  • Book your Universal hotel stay to go with the event. The proximity gives you a genuine advantage, whether you are doing a mid-night drop for younger kids or just want somewhere close to wind down after 12:30 AM.
  • Watch for early bird ticket pricing. Tickets start lower and increase with demand. If you know you are going, buy early. You can save up to 40 percent compared to buying closer to the event date.

Our Verdict

The Orlando Informer Meetup is one of the most value-packed experiences you can have at Universal Orlando. The unlimited food alone regularly covers the cost of entry when you do the real math. Add in the near-empty parks, the no-wait character meet and greets, and the exclusive access to rides like Hagrid's that are harder than ever to do on a regular day ticket, and this event stands on its own. We have done it three times. We will keep going back. And now that Epic Universe is part of the equation, the ceiling just got higher.
