Cabana Bay Beach Resort: Is This Universal's Best Value Hotel for Families?

July 6, 2026  ·  9 min read  ·  By Jordan Roberson
← Back to Blog

You have probably heard about the big three Universal hotels so many times that Cabana Bay sounds like the backup option. We walked in with that same assumption. One night later, we were asking out loud why more families are not talking about this place. Pull up a chair. We are walking you through the food, the room, the noise we did not expect, and whether Cabana Bay actually earns the title of Universal's best hotel for families.

Cabana Bay Beach Resort Overview

Say the words Universal hotel out loud and your brain probably jumps straight to the big three, the ones with Express Pass built in. Cabana Bay is not one of them, and that is exactly the assumption we walked in with. It is Universal Orlando's largest resort, and it sits in the prime value tier, which means a lower nightly rate and no Express Pass access. That is the real tradeoff, the one every family weighs before booking. Walk the property for five minutes though, and you start to understand why so many families choose it anyway.

The theming pulls you in before you even check in. Retro signage two stories tall, a classic woodie wagon parked out front like it never left the 1950s, palm trees, and a color palette that feels like a postcard from a different decade. You are easing into vacation mode before you ever reach a park gate.

We caught that exact energy on camera within thirty seconds of pulling in. See for yourself, straight from our Instagram.

Bayliner Diner: Food and Dining

Bayliner Diner is set up cafeteria style, and that alone tells you it was built for families rather than trends. Different stations mean everyone grabs something different. We walked away with a Cuban sandwich for the adults, pizza for the kids, and a lineup of international dishes we did not expect at a value resort. Picky eater sitting next to an adventurous one? Bayliner Diner does not care. Everyone eats.

Breakfast for our group came out to about $40 and up depending on family size, and Annual Pass holders get an additional 15% off on top of that. Charge it straight to your room and skip the fumbling for a card at every station.

Here is the detail that stuck with us more than anything else on this trip. Bayliner Diner has paper plates available at the self serve stations. We had genuinely never seen that offered at a hotel before. Small detail, but if you have ever tried to balance a real plate while wrangling a toddler, you already know exactly why it matters.

Roberson Family Tip

Do not skip the milkshake bar, full stop. We tried ube ice cream mixed with cookies and cream on our server's recommendation, and it might be the best combination we have had at any Universal resort. If ube is on the menu when you visit, that is your order.

The Family Suite

Picture this: kids asleep in one room, you actually awake and relaxing in another, nobody creeping around in the dark trying not to wake anyone up. That is the family suite at Cabana Bay, two queen beds plus a separate living area with a pull out sofa. That kind of separation is rare at this price point, and it is the single biggest reason we would book Cabana Bay again.

Current nightly rates at Cabana Bay generally fall between $150 and $350 depending on room type and season. Standard rooms typically start around $120 to $160 per night, while the larger family suites, which sleep up to six, usually run between $200 and $400 per night.

Roberson Family Tip

Want to level this room up even further? Splurge on the family suite with a Volcano Bay view. It is the only room type at Cabana Bay with a genuinely separated bedroom, the one that actually feels like a suite instead of an upgraded standard room. We had the pool view this trip and loved it. Next time, we are booking the view that lights up at night.

Noise and Walk to the Parks

No review is worth reading if it skips the parts that were not perfect, so here they are. Our room sat close enough to the elevator and main hallway that we could hear people coming and going most of the night. It did not ruin our stay. It is just worth knowing before you book, especially if you are a light sleeper.

The property is enormous, split across multiple towers, and yes, there is a walking path to the parks and CityWalk. It works. It is also longer than you would guess, closer to 40 minutes round trip. Take the resort bus. Your feet will thank you, especially with kids in tow.

Here is the thing though. Neither tradeoff changed how we felt about this resort. At this price point, they are easy tradeoffs to make.

Resort Amenities

This is where Cabana Bay quietly separates itself from every other hotel in its price tier. The arcade upstairs from Bayliner Diner is, without exaggeration, the biggest hotel arcade we have ever walked into, and it connects straight into the bowling alley. Eat, bowl, game, repeat, all in one loop.

Cabana Bay also runs one of the only full service Starbucks locations at any Universal resort. Most of the other hotels hand you a limited menu. If your morning depends on a real Starbucks order, that is a genuine perk worth knowing about.

The gym leans fully into the retro theme, memorabilia on the walls, old training footage playing on a loop, and it is a full sized gym with free weights. That is rare for a hotel at this price point, and it easily rivals a standard fitness chain gym.

We also stumbled onto a quiet upstairs lounge near the arcade. Comfortable seating, charging stations, and almost nobody in it both times we passed through. Consider it your reset button when the resort gets busy everywhere else.

The Pools

Cabana Bay has two separate pool areas on opposite sides of the property. One side brings a water slide, the other brings a beach style entry and the lazy river everyone talks about. Both sides have bathrooms and a changing area, so you are covered no matter which one you land on.

Here is what mattered most to us though. Zero entry design, paired with life jackets available right at the pool, means zero stress for parents like us, pun fully intended. None of our kids are strong swimmers yet, and easing into the water together without anyone feeling unsafe or overwhelmed made that pool time genuinely stress free. Watching them play in that lazy river, every one of them safe and having the time of their lives? That is a moment from this trip we are not forgetting anytime soon.

We were only there one night. The resort still made it feel like a full vacation. Between the pools, the arcade, and the food, Cabana Bay does not feel like a stopover before the parks, it feels like a destination on its own. That is exactly why we love pairing a Cabana Bay stay with park focused trips like our Orlando Informer Meetup weekends. Close to all of the action, with a resort this fun to come back to at the end of the night? Hard combination to beat.

Roberson Family Grade 5 / 5
Our Verdict

We are giving Cabana Bay Beach Resort a full 5 out of 5, and we want to be straight with you about why given everything above. Yes, you will hear some hallway noise. Yes, the walk to the parks takes longer than you would like. But at this price point, with a family suite that actually separates parents from kids, a food hall that feeds everyone well starting around $40, an arcade bigger than most family entertainment centers, and pools built for peace of mind with little swimmers, there is no other Universal hotel that delivers this much value per dollar. This is not just a good value hotel. For families, it might be the best hotel Universal has.
